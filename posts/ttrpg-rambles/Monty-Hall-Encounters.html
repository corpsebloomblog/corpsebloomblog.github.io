A small one for the first post I'm gonna make.

I don't know if theres a better or more recognisable term already in used but I call them 'Monty Hall Encounters', named after the gameshow host that the Monty Hall Problem is named after. This doesn't have anything to do with the mathematics of the problem, just the same setup. Anyways, you've probably heard of it, seen it, or seen it referenced: three doors, behind one is a prize.

There is no reason to choose any of the doors over any other. Its random.

If you give players options but not enough information that they can make a meaningful choice between them - left or right, high or low road, the woods or the mountains - then that robs them of some degree of offered player agency. Even if *you* know whats there and its not a quantum ogres situation, if to the *players* the two options are just names, and they can't actually choose what they're going up against, then you're not just offering them a choice where their choice is random (so thus they have no agency and it may as well be a coinflip or a roll for what they see), but you're literally dangling the lack of agency in front of them.

Now, unless you literally put two identical doors in front of them, the players still get *some* info. Up high in the mountains they can probably expect precarious cliffs, the forest they can expect wolves. But if you do anything INTERESTING and not stock-standard, its probably going to be stuff that players (who live in our mundane, less interesting world) wont know about or can't intuit. So give them info! Drop some rumours! Let them ask about the different options or better yet, try and give them info at the outset. If their characters would know, give them that info too!

While I use the term 'encounter' this can scale down to more fine-grain detail. If a player decides their character is about to do something risky, or very difficult, or dangerous, or that will have knock-on consequences, that their character would easily be able to forsee, but the player hasn't been told about, then they are being robbed of some of their agency if they aren't given that info - to *them*, something random and unpredictable has occurred, *and* this removes some of the immersion within their character.

Just a few rambles about player agency.
